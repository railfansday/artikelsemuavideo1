Use lunch to make human connection easier Lunch offers us much of what the front porch did: unstructured time together. No agendas, no project tasks, no scheduled meetings. This research demonstrates that while technology makes it easier to stay in contact, it doesn’t guarantee genuine relationships. Lunchtime gives employees room for conversations that aren’t tied to a project, deadline, or key performance indicator. When we protect this space, we create organic opportunities to build relationships and communities that are much more difficult to develop through structured interaction. 5. Reward outcomes instead of constant availability Employees pay attention to who and what is getting recognized. If you want to shift behavior, change what you reward. Recognition is a powerful tool for change management. Focus recognition on the outcomes and behaviors you want to encourage. The focus on outcomes becomes even more important as AI increases the speed and capacity of work. When employees can accomplish more in less time, resist the temptation to fill every available minute with more work. Be deliberate about rewarding strong outcomes rather than constant activity. Industry observers note that before I released my own organization, I worked as a web developer for a New York media company. One of my colleagues was a coding savant. They could build a webpage in mere hours, an incredible feat in the early aughts. They could identify bugs and rapidly come up with smart solutions. But they also frequently disappeared in the middle of the workday and took personal days with little to no notice. My colleague was reliable in their work, but not dependable day-to-day. Employees—and organizations—need both to provide real value. In today’s fast-changing tech landscape, customer expectations are evolving. Customers expect businesses to be reliable and digital experiences to work as promised. At the same time, they’re becoming wary of the tech race. In a recent survey of about 3,500 U.S. consumers about their digital lives, 77% said tech companies focus more on outpacing the competition than on solving real user problems. What separates leading organizations in the AI era is not reliability alone, but dependability at every step. Here’s how leaders and organizations can deliver on that promise. Make every customer experience consistent Having a product or service work the way you need it to is key. No doubt, customers take note. In the above-mentioned survey, three-quarters of consumers reported that new tech features improved their lives and that digital experiences were becoming more personalized. At the same time, fewer than 30% said they strongly agree with that sentiment. The takeaway for businesses: There’s room for improvement. Where can businesses identify those areas for improvement? One strategy is ensuring that the entire user experience is 24/7 consistent. Every interaction along the customer journey, from initial inquiries to troubleshooting, should provide the same level of care and attention. Artificial intelligence can help make that possible. Customized AI agents, for example, can respond to common inquiries around the clock. When faced with more complex or nuanced issues, they can escalate the matter to human representatives. This ensures customers can connect with your company whenever they need to, and without requiring employees to be always on. Consistency also means ensuring customers receive the same information across channels, whether they’re perusing the website, DMing with a chatbot via an app, or speaking with an employee on the phone. For customers, the result isn’t just a reliable product or service—it’s feeling heard and seen. That’s where dependability makes a difference. Customers build trust when every interaction feels responsive and aligns with what the organization has promised. Ensure every interaction feels like your brand Every interaction between customers and your company is an opportunity to build a relationship—or weaken an existing one. Especially in the age of AI, your company’s voice has to be cohesive, whether customers are chatting with an employee or a chatbot. As noted in Harvard Business Review, chatbots are becoming the frontline of customer interactions—and they can shape how customers perceive your brand, from its personality and values to its approach to customer service. What’s more, the HBR article highlights research showing that people who interacted with a warm, supportive AI companion felt closer to the chatbot than to colleagues, acquaintances, and even friends. Owners of a more task-oriented assistant, on the other hand, felt less connected to it than to their human relationships. The implication for brands: How AI communicates impacts the customer experience, as well as the kind of relationship customers believe they have with a company. For starters, customer-facing AI tools should reflect your brand identity. With a chatbot, for example, you can begin by defining its personality; whether it’s a supportive coach, an empathetic companion, or a sophisticated advisor. Decide the kind of information it handles and when conversations should be handed off to a human. AI-powered chatbots should do more than just sound like your brand. They should deliver the kind of relationship that reflects your company’s mission and values. Anticipate customer needs and act on them In the AI era, one of the biggest competitive advantages is anticipating customers’ needs, sometimes even before they’re aware of them. Consider a large language model like ChatGPT, which predicts what you might need next, encouraging users to continue engaging with it. For instance, you prompt ChatGPT to generate an outline; it does, then asks whether you’d like a draft introduction as well. Whether or not you say yes is your choice, but the option is there. Today’s companies can leverage AI to anticipate needs, streamline service, and resolve issues faster. AI-powered platforms like Chorus.ai can analyze customer interactions, synthesize sentiments, and flag recurring pain points. Then, your employees can better understand the customer experience and focus on developing solutions. Looking forward, AI-powered tools like Thematic can identify emerging trends and themes, helping companies evolve their products and services to remain competitive. As the founder of a company that began with online forms and has continually expanded its offerings over the past two decades, I know firsthand the importance of adapting to changing needs and spotting market opportunities. Importantly, AI is not error proof. Humans should always be kept in the loop to review decisions that impact the customer experience. Be transparent about AI use and jump in if and when AI falls short. That kind of behavior builds dependability and, on the customer side, reinforces loyalty.
