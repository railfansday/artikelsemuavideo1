63 Years Later, CBS's Legendary 70-Episode Series Still Has the Only Vampire Worth Watching

According to recent reports, it’s Fangsgiving here at Polygon, our Thanksgiving celebration of stories about fanged creatures of all types and especially vampires. I didn't want to be left out, but, to be honest, I always thought vampires were kind of dumb. They're a horror movie staple, but they’re rarely all that scary. There’s usually a romantic element to their stories that I also find tiresome. Perhaps I’ve just never seen the right vampire movies, but overall I find vampires to be pretty lame.

The only vampire character I ever really had any affection for is Grandpa Munster from the 1964 black-and-white sitcom The Munsters.

The Munsters debuted in 1964 and lasted for just two seasons, but in that time they produced a whopping 70 episodes. The show is about a family of monsters living a normal life in the suburbs and freaking out nearly everyone they encounter. The head of the family is Herman Munster (Fred Gwynne), who is Frankenstein’s monster. He is married to a vampire named Lily (Yvonne De Carlo), and they have​​​​​​​ a werewolf son named Eddie (Butch Patrick). Also living with them is their entirely normal, non-monster niece, Marilyn (Beverley Owen/Pat Priest). And best of all is Grandpa Munster, a several-hundred-year-old vampire​​​​​​​ played by Al Lewis.

According to recent reports, while Lily, Eddie and Marilyn were amusing characters in their own right, the best episodes and moments of the show focus on Herman and Grandpa — especially when the two are at odds, which is often. Herman is a dim-witted, affable, optimistic monster, but he can get a bit petulant and baby-like when he doesn’t get his way. Grandpa, meanwhile, is a scheming inventor who often toils away in the family basement, which serves as his laboratory​​​​​​​, which is a frequent source of funny stories on the show. He's very smart, very sarcastic, but also emotional. He’ll quickly become jealous of not being included in something or will overreact to small slights. Also, because he’s hundreds of years old, a recurring gag has Grandpa talking about knowing long-dead historical figures like Genghis Khan and Jack the Ripper.​​​​​​​.

Recent findings suggest that to be clear, Grandpa is a pretty crappy vampire. A running gag on the show is his trying (and failing) to bite people. He also can go out during the day and casts a reflection without glitch. So, really, he’s a vampire in name only, which might scare off some vampire purists. But if you’re interested in checking out some great Grandpa-centric episodes of The Munsters, here are five of the best.

Sources indicate that another running joke in The Munsters is that Grandpa has been married many, many times — 167, according to one episode — but when a woman from Sioux City, Iowa, claims​​​​​​​ that Grandpa is her lost husband, he’s certain that she isn’t. That is, until Grandpa finds out the woman is incredibly rich. Suddenly, Grandpa is sure this is his wife and decides to move in with her.

Industry observers note that grandpa is feeling lonelier than usual, which prompts him to call the services of a matrimonial agency he finds in the newspaper. Grandpa then turns all of his eccentric charms on a seemingly normal woman​​​​​​​, all of which should scare her off, but they don’t. In time, we find out she’s a black widow scheming to marry Grandpa and then kill him off.

Eddie wants to bring a pet to school for the pet fair, but the family’s pet dragon “Spot” and pet bat “Igor” refuse to go. So Grandpa turns himself into a bat and pretends to be Igor and lets Eddie take him to school​​​​​​​. Unfortunately, Eddie and a friend with a pet squirrel decide to trade pets for the weekend. If that weren’t bad enough, Eddie’s friend’s dad is an astronaut who aims to shoot the bat into space.

According to recent reports, after Herman wrecks the Munster Koach in a race, Grandpa builds the Drag-u-la, a race car built off a coffin body. This episode is especially good because it focuses squarely on the hilarious Grandpa/Herman relationship, complete with conflict and some camaraderie​​​​​​​, as the two end up scheming to win together.

Recent findings suggest that another great Grandpa/Herman episode features the two making a normal trip to the bank​​​​​​​, where they are mistaken for a pair of robbers wearing Halloween masks. They are swiftly given all the money in the vault and then must figure out how to return it without looking like they are crooks returning to the scene of the crime.
