Considering that Grand Theft Auto 6 just released after a 13-year wait, it’s safe to say that we won’t be getting another one any time soon. That’s fine, because Leonida is so enormous that I know I’ll still be finding secrets in it years from now. And yet, I can’t help myself. I’m already wondering where the series could possibly go next. Allow me to offer one unsolicited idea: Massachusetts.

According to recent reports, that’s right suckers, Miami and Los Angeles’ time is over. It’s time to let me drive a sports car down the Freedom Trail as I barrel through Boston blasting Dropkick Murphys. You can chalk this up to a little Thanksgiving homesickness, as I wasn’t able to travel to my home state for the holiday, but I am convinced I am on to something here.

It has been revealed that the thing that really makes GTA 6 so special is Leonida. Rockstar’s digital recreation of Florida is a perfect playground for GTA’s sandbox chaos, and it takes advantage of the state’s geographical diversity to do that. You’ve got a bustling Miami to build on for Vice City, but you also have a mix of run-down towns, rural farmlands, beaches, and quiet islands. That wide array of locations makes for a more varied GTA experience, as you bounce between areas with very different speeds.

In a recent development, you know what other state has that feel? Massachusetts, baby! You’ve got Boston at the heart of it all, a historic city that’s unlike any Rockstar has ever recreated. Just outside the city limits, you’ve got a host of quaint suburban towns filled with shopping malls and banks. Go west, and you’ll end up in the deep wilderness, just as you find yourself in when you go north in Leonida. On the east side, you’ve got Cape Cod, a vacation getaway built on a fishing economy. And just off the coast, you’ve got scenic islands like Martha’s Vineyard and Nantucket. Boil all of that down into a tight GTA map, and you’ve got all the same basic ingredients that make Leonida fun to explore, but in a state that looks and feels very different.

Sources indicate that rockstar is no stranger to the region, either. 2006’s Bully was set in a fictional town in Latest England. Even though that was 20 years ago, Rockstar nailed the feeling of suburban ennui that exists throughout the state. It’s easy to see how the team could recapture that spirit by including its own take on Harvard in a GTA game. Hell, why not just make the town of Bullworth canon to GTA?

It has been revealed that there’s more to Leonida than its landmarks, of course. Florida also makes for a great GTA setting because it’s full of the kind of strange Americans Rockstar loves to lampoon. GTA 6 gets to play around with conspiracy theorists, right-wing gun nuts, rich playboys, and more in its wide cultural satire.

Sources indicate that well guess what? Massachusetts is full of ripe comedic targets too! Within Boston alone, you’ve got your pick of loudmouthed bros who always seem eager to get into a bar fight. That’s contrasted with wealthy retirees living in beach bubbles, hustling Italian-Americans in Boston’s North End, artsy communities in towns like Northampton, and so much more. There’s so much to satirize beyond your typical Masshole, even though Rockstar could have a field day poking fun at the accent.

In a recent development, more than anything, I’m dying to see Boston’s obsessive sports culture make its way into GTA. You get some nods to the real sports world of Miami in GTA 6, but it would most assuredly be the central focus of a Boston-set game full of loudmouthed Red Sox and Patriots fans. Let me go to Gillette Stadium and plow through Patriot Place in a bulldozer, cowards! And considering how much Rockstar enjoys lampooning current cultural trends, there’s even fertile ground to dig into the rise of sports betting and treat it as a criminal enterprise. I mean, Rockstar must be licking its lips at the prospect of making a fake Kalshi, right?

Sources indicate that my wishlist of things I’d love to see in Grand Theft Massachusetts is long: Faneuil Hall! Boston Dynamics! Logan Airport! I’m sure that people from just about any other state in America feel the same way about their home. So much of the fun of GTA 6 is getting such a wide view of Florida that really emphasizes how diverse a single state can be. It’s easy to flatten them out, judging them by their cultural urban epicenters or voting demographics, but a good road trip will show you just how vibrant even the smallest pockets of the country can be. Every state deserves to be roasted as bad as Florida.
