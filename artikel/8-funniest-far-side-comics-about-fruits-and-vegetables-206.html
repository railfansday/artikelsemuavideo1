Decades after Gary Larson concluded his comic strip The Far Side back in 1995, fans of the series continue to read it regularly, and fresh fans are discovering it all the time. Some of Larson’s most beloved comic strips feature animals drawn in his signature style, including dogs, chickens, and especially cows. He often gave these animals human-like intelligence and other anthropomorphic traits and depicted them in very human-like scenarios.

Larson didn’t just do this with animals, however. The man had a strong surrealist streak​​​​​​​, and sometimes he’d make all sorts of things the subjects of his comic strips​​​​​​​. This includes fruits and vegetables, which he often paired with a bit of wordplay or some common association with the given food group.

In a recent development, here are the eight funniest Far Side comics featuring fruits and vegetables. You’re also welcome to snack while enjoying them.

Sources indicate that larson began publishing The Far Side on Jan. 1, 1980, making this one of his earliest comics, when it appeared only in The San Francisco Chronicle​​​​​​​. It depicts a frustrated carrot threatening his kids with a terrifying story of how actual carrots are treated.

One of Larson’s signature moves in The Far Side was to take a well-known idiom and interpret it in the most literal way possible. Here, the biblical phrase “forbidden fruit” gets twisted into a comic where a lime is trying to enter a fruit-themed nightclub which doesn’t allow anyone who’s green.

Recent findings suggest that on second thought, I’m not quite sure if that’s a lime, as it only vaguely resembles one. I don’t recall ever seeing a lime with a stem like that. Perhaps it’s a guava? Or a green plum? I’d say maybe a pear, but it isn’t at all pear-shaped. Perhaps the fruit is some other kind of fruit that isn’t yet ripe​​​​​​​, which means it could be a whole bunch of things. And maybe that’s part of the joke, as he’s not yet ripe enough for the adult nightclub? I’m really not sure.

I’m also unsure whether the fruits in this comic are​​​​​​​ anthropomorphic fruit or if they are people dressed like fruit. Just look at the human-like arms of the banana. That, to me, suggests that the banana is a man in a banana suit, yet his face is more fruit-like.

Industry observers note that carrots seem to be a particular favorite of Larson’s as they show up more often than any other vegetable​​​​​​​ (except maybe potatoes). Here, two “Carrots of the evening” try to pick up a passing celery​​​​​​​ as a customer. While we don’t know whether he accepts the offer​​​​​​​, I’m thinking he does and there’s nothing wrong with that. Prostitution should be legal and I don’t see a ring on that celery’s finger.

Sources indicate that mr. Potato Head appeared in a handful of Gary Larson comics​​​​​​​, but this darkly funny one is easily the best. It depicts Mrs. Potato Head on a witness stand, testifying against her husband who had chased her around with a “Veg-o-matic.” I especially love how furious Mr. Potato Head looks here. Even Don Rickles’ Mr. Potato Head from the Toy Story films never had eyes quite this angry.

In a recent development, nowadays, for the most part, vegetarians are fairly common, but in the 1980s and 1990s, when Gary Larson was still doing The Far Side, it was far less “normal.” As a result, vegetarians became punchlines​​​​​​​ in a number of Larson's comic strips. This one is a great example, in which​​​​​​​ a bunch of “Early vegetarian” cave people triumphantly carry back a carrot. I especially love the detail of them carrying shovels as their weapons.
