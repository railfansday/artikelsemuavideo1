Sources indicate that stephen King’s books have inspired countless adaptations over the years. Several have even been adapted for the screen more than once, including Firestarter (1984 and 2022) and The Running Man (1987 and 2025).

Sources indicate that however, one film adaptation stands out for its distinctive impact on the horror genre. That film is Brian De Palma’s 1976 supernatural horror classic, Carrie, featuring Sissy Spacek as our titular protagonist. And while its story has been adapted multiple times in both film and TV, the original remains unbeatable.

According to recent reports, with Carrie leaving HBO Max on Nov. 30, there's never been a better time to hide from your family this Thanksgiving and spend the afternoon watching a young woman pushed to the brink and unleashing supernatural violence.

Sources indicate that king's story follows awkward 16-year-old Carrie White as she faces torment at home and at school. At home, her abusive mother’s religious fanaticism creates an oppressive environment. At school, a group of popular girls targets her. When Carrie gets her first period, her main bully, Chris (Nancy Allen), cruelly humiliates her and encourages the girls to pelt her with tampons. Carrie’s teacher, Miss Collins (Betty Buckley), punishes Chris and bars her from attending prom. Furious, Chris begins plotting revenge against Carrie.

Sources indicate that however, Carrie’s discovery comes with a frightening catch: Whenever she becomes upset, she can move objects with her mind. As she struggles to understand and control this mysterious ability, Chris carries out her plan to humiliate Carrie in front of everyone at prom. The two events collide, turning the night into a bloodbath as Carrie unleashes an unprecedented wave of vengeance against her tormentors, including her mother.

Sources indicate that over the years, Stephen King has never hesitated to speak out about adaptations of his books that, in his view, stray from the original meaning. One well-known example is Stanley Kubrick’s The Shining, which King fans know he has criticized so harshly that he once described the film as “a massive, beautiful Cadillac with no engine inside it.”.

King feels quite differently about De Palma’s Carrie. In an interview with Cinefantastique, he praised De Palma’s take on his book, even though the film is far more satirical than the novel.

Recent findings suggest that “Sissy Spacek was excellent, but right behind her — in a smaller part than it should have been — was John Travolta,” King said. “He played the part of Billy Nolan the way I wish I’d written it, half-funny and half-crazy.”.

Sources indicate that over the years, King’s Carrie has been adapted in many ways, though not all for film. The latest adaptation is by Mike Flanagan, known for Midnight Mass, and has generally been well received, with an 85% Tomatometer score on Rotten Tomatoes.

Yet, as beloved as Flanagan’s adaptation is, horror fans owe it to themselves to watch the classic that debuted the Carrie franchise. For one thing, King was absolutely right to praise Spacek, who captures Carrie’s vulnerability with heartbreaking precision while also conveying her rage through subtle yet striking physical gestures. Even a single twitch of her eye makes you stand up straight, waiting for her to snap.

More importantly, as the first screen adaptation of King’s work to achieve both critical and commercial success (it earned $33.8 million at the box office on a modest $1.8 million budget), Carrie undoubtedly helped make King a household name in horror. It showed that his work could translate well to the significant screen and paved the way for later cinematic classics like The Shining, Cujo, and Christine.

With such monumental success behind it, the only question we’ve got is this: Why aren’t you watching Carrie right now? Go on, git.

Carrie is available to watch now exclusively on HBO Max. But don’t wait too long; it leaves the streamer on Nov. 30.
