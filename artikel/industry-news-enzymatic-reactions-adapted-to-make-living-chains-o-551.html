Industry News: Enzymatic reactions adapted to make ‘living’ chains of sugars

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that long chains of sugars known as polysaccharides support many biological functions, from strengthening tissues to modulating cell signalling. Synthesizing these compounds in the laboratory with precise lengths and architectures, which are needed to establish how the structures of polysaccharides affect biological behaviour, has been difficult. Writing in Nature, Yin et al.1 now report an enzyme-based strategy that produces highly uniform polysaccharides and enables chains to restart growth after consuming all of the sugar building blocks in the reaction, once more sugars are added. The authors achieved this control by adjusting how readily an enzyme binds the small sugar molecules that are used to initiate the growth of each chain.
