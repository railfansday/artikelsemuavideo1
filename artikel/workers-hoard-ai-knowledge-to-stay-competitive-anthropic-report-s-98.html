Workers hoard AI knowledge to stay competitive, Anthropic report says

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that AI tools are becoming increasingly ubiquitous, but the best techniques for using them are likely still a secret. This is because workers who develop highly effective AI tricks have no incentive to share them with their colleagues or their bosses, notes a report published Monday by Anthropic. Researchers interviewed 66 executives, managers, and employees at 58 of Anthropic’s corporate customers and found that many workers are reluctant to share their best AI tricks, citing reasons ranging from job security to more leisure time. The practice has frustrated managers looking to exploit AI-driven efficiencies. The report quotes one exasperated executive saying, “They don’t want you to know, because they want to use those 10 hours a week to go walk their dog.” AI hoarding is a new form of the age-old workplace phenomenon of knowledge hiding, in which employees deliberately withhold information from their coworkers to retain a competitive edge. Employees in the AI age have even more at stake: their jobs. If the trick they develop is powerful enough, they might render themselves redundant. And even for those whose jobs are secure, revealing a time-saving trick might just mean they are assigned more work. That raises a thorny question for business leaders: How do you incentivize employees to help automate their own jobs? Employers hoping to discourage AI hoarding may need to rethink how they measure and reward productivity. Otherwise, employees might realize that their most valuable professional asset is something they’re better off keeping secret.
