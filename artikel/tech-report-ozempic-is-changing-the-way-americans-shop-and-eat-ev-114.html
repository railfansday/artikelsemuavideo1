Tech Report: Ozempic is changing the way Americans shop and eat, even if they’ve never taken it

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The latest class of drugs led by Ozempic and Wegovy don’t just have transformative effects on the people who take them – they’re reshaping the lives around them too. Patients prescribed GLP-1s are eating less and drinking less, but those behavioral shifts are trickling down to people in their life who aren’t taking the medications themselves. Nearly one in four U.S. consumers have adjusted the way that they shop, eat, and drink based on knowing other people who take GLP-1s, according to a new survey from market research firm Escalent. The survey homed in on a group that the market research group calls “echo consumers,” which refers to people who don’t take a GLP-1 themselves but are changing their habits based on others in their social circles who do. The survey included data from 315 U.S. and 335 U.K. respondents who haven’t taken a GLP-1 drug but know someone who does and report changes to their own behavior based on those social connections. “Much of the attention surrounding GLP-1 medications has focused on the people taking them, and those living in their households,” Escalent Europe Managing Director Melissa Bane wrote. “… For retailers, food and beverage brands and restaurants, that’s a significant consumer group whose changing habits have gone largely unnoticed.” Among the echo consumers, 42% reported exercising more, 36% mentioned they consume more wellness products, and 27% are drinking less. When eating out, 49% said they are ordering less at restaurants when eating with someone taking a GLP-1, while 21% are opting for alternative activities instead of socializing while eating and drinking. Almost one in five say they have picked different gifts for GLP-1 users they’ve shopped for, and many say they are buying different food and drinks to bring along to social gatherings in light of smaller appetites and friends and family who aren’t drinking as much. Industry-shifting numbers While the new survey only offers a small snapshot of consumer behavior, GLP-1 medications are clearly causing some seismic cultural shifts that we’re only beginning to understand. In the U.S., a massive 11% of the population are currently prescribed GLP-1s and 15% say they have tried them for weight loss before. That huge swath of Americans is already walking, running, and cycling more, buying fewer sweet treats, spending less on groceries, and drinking less alcohol. Habit shifts among GLP-1 users alone already represent a massive amount of change for industries to adapt to across just a few short years. Beyond those consumers, Escalent’s survey suggests that there are 1.7 times as many people shifting their behavior in light of the widespread use of weight management drugs than there are GLP-1 users. If similar numbers play out in future research, that’s an even more massive chunk of the population suddenly eating, drinking, and buying differently than they did prior to the advent of drugs like Ozempic, Wegovy, and Zepbound. “For Echo Consumers, a box of chocolates or a bottle of wine may no longer be the gift of choice, and social occasions could increasingly revolve around activities rather than going out for food and drink,” Bane said, calling the changes a “major shift” that will be even more evident during the holiday season. “Brands, retailers and hospitality businesses will need to find ways to adapt their offerings to reflect changing demand as these habits continue to spread through social networks.”.
